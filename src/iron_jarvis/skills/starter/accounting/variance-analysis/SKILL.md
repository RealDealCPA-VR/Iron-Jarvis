---
name: variance-analysis
description: >-
  Compare actual results with a budget or a prior period and explain the differences that matter.
---

Use this when the user wants to know why numbers changed between periods or against a budget.

1. Line up the two sets of figures by account. Say what each column is and the period it covers.
2. For every line give the change in money and in percent. Calculate them with a tool or formulas; never by eye.
3. Pick the lines that matter: the largest changes in money first, then any line that moved more than the user's threshold (ask, or use 10% and say so).
4. For each line that matters, give the reason only if the data shows it (volume, price, timing, a one-off, a reclassification). If the data does not show the reason, write "cause not shown in the data" and say what would show it.
5. Separate timing differences from real changes.
6. End with three to five plain sentences a business owner could read, and the questions that are still open.
