---
name: customer-persona
description: >-
  Describe a typical customer from real information: who they are, what they want and what stops them.
---

Use this when the user wants to understand or describe who they are selling to.

1. Build it from evidence the user gives: customer lists, reviews, sales notes, survey answers. Say what you used.
2. Write one page:
   - a short label for this customer (not a made-up name and life story),
   - their situation and what triggers them to look for help,
   - what they want to achieve,
   - what worries them or stops them,
   - how they decide and who else has a say,
   - where they look for information,
   - the words they use for the problem.
3. Mark each point as "from the data" or "assumption".
4. If there is little evidence, say so and list the five questions to ask real customers.
5. Do not describe people by age, race, sex or income unless the data shows it and it matters to the work.
